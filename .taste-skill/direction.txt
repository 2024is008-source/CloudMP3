This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The whole page is a board of tiles fitted edge to edge with narrow gaps, like a tidy desk seen from above. Each tile is one thing: the name, a statement, a photograph, a number, a link, a short list. There is no hero and no sequence of bands; the page is one continuous composition that the eye roams. It suits subjects with many small true things to show (a studio with work, writing, clients and news; a product with several distinct capabilities) and it replaces the generic bento by having real content in every tile and a strict system for sizes.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with 8px gaps and 8–12px outer margins, filled completely, no empty cells. Tiles use only four sizes (for example 6×4, 6×2, 3×2, 3×4 in column × row units of about 90px). Every tile is a flat filled rectangle: ground colour one step from the page, or a palette colour, or a photograph covering it. One radius everywhere (0, or 6–10px). The first screen contains: a name tile (the name at display size, the largest type on the page) with the wordmark set large, a statement tile with the description at 28–40px, two or three image tiles and one action tile in the accent colour. Text inside tiles is top-left aligned with 20–24px padding and never centred. Below the first screen the board continues with the same sizes; the last row is the footer made of tiles.

## 3. Type — Switzer
One neo-grotesk doing everything: semibold headings pulled tight, small regular text, no capitals and no second voice. Suits software, hardware and studio pages that want to look exact and unshowy.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=switzer@400,500,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Switzer** — weight 600 · tracking -0.04em · leading 0.95 · sentence case; charcoal rather than pure black
- Text: **Switzer** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking -0.005em
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
One grotesk in two weights. Inside tiles: a large size for single statements, a small size for lists. No uppercase labels in tile corners.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Page ground, tile ground one step lighter or darker, and one or two palette colours on selected tiles. Photographs supply the rest.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node D:/code-archive-unpacker-main(2)/code-archive-unpacker-main/.qoder/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "link"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Photographs fill whole tiles with `object-fit: cover`; choose images that crop well to both wide and tall tiles.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Name band and rails
One viewport filled completely with tiles on 8px gaps and 8px margins. Across the top, a band tile 170px tall holds the name on one line at clamp(140px, 14.4vw, 220px), heavy, tracking -0.04em, centred and deliberately wider than the band so both ends are cropped by the tile's edges (overflow: hidden, white-space: nowrap). Under it a row of four equal tiles, 64px tall, each holding one text link at 16px, top-left aligned. The remaining 62% of the height is three columns. Left rail (columns 1-2): two stacked tiles, a portrait photograph above and a short list of five facts below. Centre (columns 3-10): one large photograph tile, object-fit: cover, with a statement tile in the ground tone laid along its bottom third holding one sentence at clamp(24px, 2.4vw, 36px), top-left aligned on 24px padding. Right rail (columns 11-12): a full-height stack of five flat colour tiles, a light-to-dark run from the palette; the lowest takes the accent colour and holds the single action, the others each carry one short fact at 15px.

On a phone: Tiles restack in two columns with 6px gaps. The name band stays full width: 26vw type cropped at both sides in a 110px band. The four link tiles become a two-by-two block. The large photograph tile follows at full width, 4:3, with the statement tile under it at 6vw. The colour rail becomes a horizontal row of five short tiles, the action tile last and twice as wide.
Headline: The name on one cropped line, any length, plus one sentence of up to 60 characters in the statement tile.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. how-it-works — Stages as one bar (band)
The product's progress bar, enlarged until it spans the page, is the diagram. One bar runs across columns 1–12, 22vh tall, built as a flex row of three or four solid segments with 6px gaps, root marked data-ui. Each segment's width follows how long that stage really takes (flex-grow from the true durations, 12% minimum), and each is a different flat palette colour, so the reader sees at once that signing up is a sliver and the rest is the product working. Inside every segment, top-left with 20px padding, its duration in words at clamp(20px, 2vw, 30px): 'Two minutes', 'About a day'. Under each segment, on its left edge, one clause at 17px, 22ch wide, beginning with its order word. Above the bar, 64px clear of it, the heading: two to five words at clamp(48px, 6.4vw, 104px), flush left. Nothing else.
On a phone: The bar turns vertical: segments stack as full-width blocks whose heights keep the proportions, 72px minimum, 6px gaps, about 70svh in total. Each holds its duration at 22px top-left and its clause at 16px beneath. The heading sits above at 11vw on two lines.

### 2. showcase — Rows as a board (table)
The product's main list is set like a station departure board. Eight to ten real rows (transactions, issues, deploys, bookings) run the full viewport width inside 12px side margins. Each row is a filled band 72px tall, one tone off a dark ground, separated from the next by a 4px gap and never by a line; the whole board is one fragment marked data-ui. Text inside is 26–30px: the name from column 1, two or three facts at columns 6, 8 and 10, and at column 12 a status word on a solid block of its status colour; figures use tabular numerals. The section's top and bottom edges cut the first and last rows in half, so the list seems to continue. The fourth row is open: 216px tall, in the accent colour, with its detail in two lines at 17px. Clicking another row opens that one instead. No heading: the open row's name is the largest type.
On a phone: Rows keep the 4px gaps and are 64px tall inside 8px margins: the name at 20px on the left, the status block on the right, the other facts dropped. The open row is 200px tall and lists the dropped facts as three lines at 16px. Six rows show; the first and last are cropped.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 3. features — Staggered fragment cells (grid)
A three-column grid of six filled cells with 8px gaps, each cell 420–460px tall and filled one tone off the ground, no outlines. Every cell has two parts: a text part (a title at 17px medium and three lines at 15px in the soft ink, 24px padding) and a real fragment of the interface at true size (a control, a menu, a table slice, a swatch strip) docked flush against the opposite edge of the cell and cropped by it. The stagger is the idea: across the first row cells run text-top, fragment-top, text-top; the second row runs the reverse, so fragments form a checkerboard. Fragments differ in shape and size on purpose. The heading, two to four words at 32–40px, sits left-aligned above the grid with 56px beneath it and no supporting line.
On a phone: One column of cells at their natural height with 6px gaps. Every cell runs text first, fragment second, but fragments alternate between docking to the left edge and the right edge, and they are allowed to run off the side of the cell at true size instead of shrinking.

### 4. faq — Accordion of filled tiles (stack)
A two-part row. Columns 1–4 hold the heading, two to four words at about 4vw, `position: sticky; top: 96px` so it stays while the list scrolls past. Columns 5–12 hold the accordion: each question is a filled tile one step from the page ground, with 8px between tiles, no border and the page's one radius (or its motif's corner). A closed tile is 72px tall with the question at 20px medium on the left, 24px padding, and on the right a plus sign drawn from two 3px bars 16px long. The open tile switches to the accent colour with its assigned ink, the plus becomes a minus by rotating one bar, and the answer appears at 17px, no wider than 52ch. Build each tile as `<details>` with a shared `name` so one opens at a time. Six to nine tiles.
On a phone: The heading sits above at 9vw, not sticky. Tiles run full width with 6px gaps, questions at 18px wrapping to two lines, closed height at least 64px. The open tile keeps the accent colour, and answers are set at 16px with 20px padding.

### 5. footer — The mark, built large (band)
On a flat colour field, the brand closes the page as a construction rather than as set type. Across the full content width and about 34svh tall, the wordmark is built from blocks: each letter is knocked out of its own solid rectangle in a palette colour, the rectangles differ in height (60–100% of the band) and butt together with 4px gaps, bottoms on one line. Where the project has a simple geometric mark or motif, enlarge that instead, drawn as inline SVG from flat rectangles, circles and quarter-arcs with the same 4px gaps. Below, 56px lower: four columns of plain links at 15px across columns 1–8, and two addresses or contact lines in columns 9–12. A last row carries the copyright at the left and one small filled button at the right. Space and the block construction do all the separating.
On a phone: The block wordmark spans the full width at about 22svh tall; a long name breaks into two rows of blocks. Link columns go two across at 15px, contact lines beneath at full width, then copyright and the small button on one row.

## 8. Motion — Edge-to-edge marquee
One line of very large type travels sideways across the full width at a slow constant speed, as a band between two sections or as the closing statement. Linear timing, duplicated content for a seamless loop, paused on hover and under reduced motion. One marquee per page, carrying real words (the name, the claim), never a list of buzzwords.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The fit. A full board with consistent gaps and varied tile contents is satisfying in the way a packed suitcase is.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Tiles are filled, never outlined, and never carry shadows. Do not put an icon and a two-line feature blurb in every tile; vary what tiles hold. Decide the phone layout as its own board: two columns, tiles re-ordered so the name and statement lead.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
